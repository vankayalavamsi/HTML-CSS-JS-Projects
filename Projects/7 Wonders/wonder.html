Great Wall of China

A massive series of fortifications stretching over 21,000 kilometers, built over many centuries to protect Chinese states from northern invasions.

While often thought of as a single wall, it is actually a network of walls and trenches built by multiple dynasties, with the most well-known sections constructed during the Ming Dynasty (1368–1644). It is the longest structure humans have ever built.

The wall traverses highly diverse landscapes, from the Gobi Desert to rugged mountain peaks. It was built using local materials—rammed earth in the plains and heavy stone bricks in the mountains. Watchtowers and garrison stations were placed at regular intervals to send smoke signals and house troops. Today, it stands as a profound symbol of Chinese civilization's persistence and architectural ambition.

LocationNorthern China
Construction7th Century BC – 1644 AD
Length21,196 kilometers
MaterialStone, brick, rammed earth, wood
FunctionBorder defense, troop garrisoning, trade control
China • 7th Century BC

Petra

A breathtaking ancient city carved directly into vibrant rose-red desert cliffs by the Nabataean people over two millennia ago.

Established as the capital of the Nabataean Kingdom around 312 BC, Petra was a crucial junction for the silk, spice, and other trade routes that linked China, India, and southern Arabia with Egypt, Greece, and Rome. The city is famous for its sophisticated water conduit system, which allowed it to thrive in the arid desert.

Accessed through a narrow, 1.2-kilometer-long gorge called the Siq, the city reveals its most famous facade, Al-Khazneh (The Treasury), at the very end. Beyond the Treasury lies a vast archaeological park containing hundreds of rock-cut tombs, temples, a Roman-style theater, and monasteries, showcasing a blend of Nabataean, Greek, and Roman architectural styles.

LocationMa'an Governorate, Jordan
Construction~312 BC
Height (Treasury)~40 meters
MaterialRose-red sandstone rock
FunctionCapital city, trade hub, tombs
Jordan • 312 BC

Christ the Redeemer

An immense Art Deco statue of Jesus Christ perched atop the 700-meter-high Mount Corcovado, overlooking Rio de Janeiro.

Completed in 1931, the statue stands 30 meters tall with an arm span of 28 meters. It is made of reinforced concrete and clad in over six million soapstone tiles. Designed by Polish-French sculptor Paul Landowski and built by Brazilian engineer Heitor da Silva Costa, it was chosen as a symbol of peace following World War I.

Sitting at the peak of Corcovado mountain in the Tijuca National Park, the statue offers 360-degree panoramic views of Rio de Janeiro, including the famous Sugarloaf Mountain, Copacabana beach, and Guanabara Bay. It has survived numerous lightning strikes due to its height and is regularly restored to maintain its striking appearance against the Brazilian sky.

LocationRio de Janeiro, Brazil
Construction1922 – 1931 AD
Height30 meters (plus 8m pedestal)
MaterialReinforced concrete, soapstone
FunctionReligious statue, symbol of peace
Brazil • 1931 AD

Machu Picchu

A spectacular 15th-century Inca citadel set high in the Andes Mountains, renowned for its sophisticated dry-stone walls and panoramic vistas.

Believed to have been built as an estate for the Inca emperor Pachacuti around 1450 AD, Machu Picchu sits at 2,430 meters above sea level. It was abandoned just over a century later during the Spanish Conquest. Because the Spanish never found it, the site remained largely intact, hidden by the dense tropical forest until American historian Hiram Bingham brought it to international attention in 1911.

The site is divided into agricultural terracing and an urban sector featuring temples, palaces, and storage rooms. The Intihuatana stone, precisely carved to mark the exact dates of the equinoxes, demonstrates the Incas' advanced astronomical knowledge. The dry-stone walls are built without mortar; the stones are cut so precisely that a knife blade cannot fit between them.

LocationCusco Region, Peru
Construction~1450 AD
Altitude2,430 meters above sea level
MaterialGranite dry-stone walls
FunctionRoyal estate, astronomical observatory
Peru • 1450 AD

Chichen Itza

A large pre-Columbian archaeological site built by the Maya civilization, famous for its massive stepped pyramid temple, El Castillo.

El Castillo (The Temple of Kukulcan) is a masterpiece of Maya-Toltec architecture. It stands 30 meters high and features 365 steps—one for each day of the solar year. During the spring and autumn equinoxes, the sun casts a shadow that creates the illusion of a feathered serpent slithering down the northern staircase, a testament to the Maya's incredible astronomical and mathematical precision.

Beyond the pyramid, the site contains a massive ball court where ancient Mesoamerican ball games were played, the Sacred Cenote (a natural sinkhole used for offerings and sacrifices), and the Temple of Warriors. Chichen Itza served as the political, economic, and religious center of the Maya civilization in the Yucatan Peninsula before its decline in the 15th century.

LocationYucatan, Mexico
Construction~600 AD
Height (El Castillo)30 meters
MaterialLimestone
FunctionReligious center, astronomical calendar
Mexico • 600 AD

Roman Colosseum

An oval amphitheater in the heart of Rome, the largest ever built, representing the peak of ancient Roman engineering and entertainment.

Completed in 80 AD under Emperor Titus, the Colosseum could hold between 50,000 and 80,000 spectators. It was the epicenter of Roman entertainment, hosting gladiatorial contests, public spectacles, mock sea battles, animal hunts, and dramas based on Classical mythology. The exterior features 80 arched entrances, allowing for rapid crowd movement.

Beneath the wooden arena floor lay the hypogeum, a complex underground network of tunnels and cages where wild animals and gladiators were held. The Colosseum even featured a retractable awning called the velarium, operated by sailors, to shield spectators from the sun. Despite being damaged by earthquakes and stone-robbers over the centuries, it remains an iconic symbol of Imperial Rome's power.

LocationRome, Italy
Construction70 – 80 AD
Height48 meters
MaterialTravertine limestone, volcanic rock, concrete
FunctionAmphitheater for gladiatorial games
Italy • 80 AD

Taj Mahal

An ivory-white marble mausoleum on the south bank of the Yamuna river, widely regarded as the finest example of Mughal architecture.

Commissioned in 1632 by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan to house the tomb of his favorite wife, Mumtaz Mahal, construction took 22 years and employed over 20,000 artisans from across Asia. The complex is laid out in a perfect bilateral symmetry, centered around a raised marble pool that reflects the image of the mausoleum.

The central dome reaches a height of 73 meters and is flanked by four smaller domes and towering minarets. The exterior is adorned with intricate inlay work of semi-precious stones (pietra dura), featuring floral motifs and calligraphy from the Quran. The translucent white marble changes color throughout the day—shimmering pink in the morning, glowing white in the evening, and appearing golden under moonlight.

LocationAgra, India
Construction1632 – 1653 AD
Height73 meters
MaterialWhite marble, semi-precious stones
FunctionMausoleum for Mumtaz Mahal
India • 1632 AD
